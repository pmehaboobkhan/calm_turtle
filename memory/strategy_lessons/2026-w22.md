## Lesson 1 — Two consecutive daily-loss events from the same macro driver (energy/crude)

**Source:** Both the 2026-05-26 and 2026-05-29 daily-loss breaches were driven by the same macro catalyst: US-Iran diplomatic de-escalation / Strait of Hormuz reopening prospects → WTI crude -5% on 05-26, -5% further on 05-29 → XOM -5.5% intraday (05-26) and -9.06% intraday (05-29).

**Observation:** XOM (~15% of equity as a single large-cap momentum name) consumed ~$750+ of the $500/0.5% daily-loss budget in isolation on each day. The position's -10% rotation stop ($142.13) was never formally breached (05-29 low ~$145.37, +2.25% cushion), but the daily-loss limit — which is a portfolio-level, multi-name, combined P&L cap — fired twice from one name's macro beta.

**Pattern for future use:** When the same macro theme (geopolitical risk premium in energy) has already caused a daily-loss breach, the surviving position's residual risk is higher than the mechanical cushion suggests. On the day following a macro-driven daily-loss event, if the underlying macro driver (crude oil, in this case) has not stabilized, the pre-close overnight-risk overlay should re-evaluate the affected position at reduced sizing or with a tighter intraday threshold — not wait for the -10% rotation stop to fire.

**Implication for re-entry:** On 2026-06-01, XOM re-enters the signal universe (rank 2, +28.43% 6m). Given two consecutive energy-beta daily-loss events, sizing should be halved on re-entry (~7.5% instead of ~15%) until crude stabilizes or one clean session confirms the energy thesis holds. Flag for next pre_market.

## Lesson 2 — GLD permanent overlay should potentially be exempt from the daily-loss de-risk mandate

**Source:** GLD was closed on 2026-05-29 pre_close at +$215 (+1.45%) under the `halt_after_daily_limit_breach=true` all-positions close mandate. GLD was the only genuinely defensive asset in the book, designed to absorb exactly the kind of macro/geopolitical shock that triggered the halt.

**Observation:** The all-positions de-risk mandate makes sense for the momentum strategy names (CSCO, GOOGL, UNH, XOM) where the same risk-off macro move that hurt the portfolio may also hurt the underlying theses. For GLD, the opposite is true: a risk-off macro event (energy selloff, geopolitical stress) is the very scenario the permanent gold overlay is designed to hedge. Closing it under the halt converts a hedge into a momentum close — contradicting the strategy design.

**Contrast with CSCO:** CSCO also produced a win (+$456) and was also closed by the mandate. CSCO's thesis (tech networking, AI infrastructure demand) is not directly correlated with geopolitical macro events, so closing it under the halt is more defensible (removed a non-hedge risk asset during stress).

**Implication:** A future version of `halt_after_daily_limit_breach=true` processing should potentially skip the position tagged with `strategy: gold_permanent_overlay` (or any `strategy: <overlay>` with `exempt_from_halt: true` in strategy_rules.yaml). This is a policy/config question requiring human review — the agent is flagging it here, not implementing it.

**Evidence logged in:** `memory/prediction_reviews/2026-05-29.md` (GLD close open question); `journals/weekly/2026-22.md` §8 lesson 3.

## Lesson 3 — Stale daily bars blocked entries for 2+ consecutive sessions (data pipeline reliability)

**Source:** 2026-05-27 EOD had bars stamped 2026-05-26 (~26h stale); 2026-05-28 EOD had bars stamped 2026-05-27 (~44.8h stale). Both sessions: AMZN entry REJECTED on stale data per risk_limits.yaml `max_data_staleness_seconds = 60`.

**Observation:** The correct outcome (NO_TRADE on stale data) was enforced, but the staleness was discovered at EOD — after the deterministic engine ran on outdated ranks. A pre_market freshness check (writing a risk event if bars are >4h stale at the pre_market routine time, which runs ~6-7 AM ET = many hours before EOD) would:
1. Alert the operator before EOD so the data issue can be triaged before the entry window
2. Prevent two consecutive silent "monitoring-only" sessions where the entry signal may have been valid

**Pattern for future use:** The pre_market routine should include a `data_freshness_check` step: if `latest_bar_age_seconds > 14400` (4 hours after prior session close), write `logs/risk_events/<ts>_stale_bar_feed.md` and notify URGENT (not just warn). The EOD guard is the backstop, not the primary alarm.

**Two-session streak note:** A single stale-bar session may be a transient network issue. Two consecutive stale-bar sessions suggests a pipeline-level failure (cache expiry, API rate limit, credential rotation, etc.) that requires investigation. The 05-28 discovery was on "44.8x over the 60s cap" — not a marginal staleness, a multi-hour feed failure.

## Lesson 4 — PENDING_BROKER log artifact blocking CB writes (W21 lesson 3, severity escalated)

**Source:** WMT BUY (05-18, closed 05-20) and NVDA BUY (05-22, closed 05-26) remain `PENDING_BROKER` in the append-only `log.csv`, so `pending_broker_count()` returns >0. The CB equity write is gated behind `pending_broker_count() == 0`, so it has been skipped for every routine since 05-26. By EOD 05-29, `pending_broker_count = 7` (added 5 close submits from today).

**W21 status:** Identified as "MUST-FIX (operational reliability)" in `memory/strategy_lessons/2026-w21.md`.

**W22 escalation:** The defect has now been silent for 2 full weeks and 10+ routines. CB peak-tracking is being maintained manually ($104,090.72 peak, last verified at 05-26 midday). If the CB state file diverges from reality due to a manual tracking error, downstream logic may read incorrect state. No genuine CB transition would have fired this week (DD max 4.86% < 8% FULL→HALF trigger), but the guard's purpose is to catch transitions automatically.

**Root cause (confirmed):** The `PENDING_BROKER` status was designed as an in-flight indicator. After broker confirms fills (order status = FILLED), the local log should be updated to `OPEN` (for buys) or `CLOSED` (for sell-closes). But `log.csv` is append-only; the initial `PENDING_BROKER` row is never retroactively updated. `pending_broker_count()` counts any row with `status=PENDING_BROKER`, so it grows with every round-trip trade.

**Required fix:** Either: (a) `pending_broker_count()` cross-checks broker order status for each `PENDING_BROKER` row and returns only count of rows where broker order is NOT in a terminal state (FILLED, CANCELED, EXPIRED); or (b) an EOD finalize sweep appends a `status=CLOSED` / `status=OPEN` row for each confirmed fill. Option (a) is simpler; option (b) is cleaner for audit trails. Requires human PR.

## Lesson 5 — NVDA same-day-fill/close validates low-persistence rank-5 thesis

**Source:** NVDA was submitted 05-22 EOD (rank 5), filled 05-26 open at $215.91 (alpaca-mirror basis, vs $197.559 original sim entry), demoted to rank-9 by the 05-26 midday signal run, and closed at 05-26 pre_close at $214.34 as the "weakest-thesis de-risk" name under the daily-loss mandate.

**Observation:** The W21 lesson 5 noted the 0.42pp NVDA/AMZN gap as "especially low-persistence." This prediction was exactly correct: NVDA fell to rank-9 within one session of its fill. The same-day-fill/close is an artifact of the short tenure, but the underlying lesson is correct:
- Rank-5 entries with <0.5pp gap to rank-6 should be treated as the weakest thesis in the book.
- When a de-risk event requires selecting the "weakest conviction" name for the first close, the most recently entered rank-5 boundary name is the correct priority choice.
- The +$453 realized (vs original entry) suggests the thesis was briefly valid; the entry was not a mistake. But the risk/reward of holding a borderline rank-5 name through a daily-loss event is asymmetric: it's the first out, not the last.

**Pattern for future de-risk events:** When executing a partial de-risk (not full liquidation), prioritize closing in this order: (1) highest unrealized loss; OR (2) lowest rank; OR (3) most recent entry. In this case, NVDA was rank-9 (criterion 2) AND most recent entry (criterion 3), which converged on the correct choice. The manual judgment on 05-26 pre_close was sound and should be made explicit in the pre_close de-risk decision logic.
