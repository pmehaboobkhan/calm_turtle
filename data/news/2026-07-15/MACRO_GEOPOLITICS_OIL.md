**Source:** [Al Jazeera — Strait of Hormuz traffic plunges](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/7/10/strait-of-hormuz-shipping-grinds-to-halt-as-us-iran-resume-fighting)  
**Timestamp:** 2026-07-10–07-14  
**1-line Summary:** Shipping traffic 50%+ below normal; 20% of global oil supply at risk if Strait remains closed for weeks.

**Tone:** BEARISH (supply shock tail risk)

**Rationale:** Historical precedent: 1973 Yom Kippur War oil embargo + 1980–1990 Iran-Iraq War saw oil spikes 50–100% over weeks. Current Strait disruption (50% traffic reduction, not full closure) is less severe, but trajectory matters. **If conflict persists and shipping avoidance continues, oil could sustainably trade $90+ for weeks.** This would re-trigger inflation narrative and force Fed to reassess dovish repricing.

## Policy Uncertainty: Trump Blockade & 20% Fee

**Headline:** President Trump announced reimposition of US naval blockade against Iranian ships and demanded that all ships crossing Strait of Hormuz pay US 20% of cargo value as "reimbursement for protection." Unprecedented policy move (no recent precedent for explicit cargo tax).

**Source:** (Derived from CNBC, Al Jazeera multiple references to Trump statements)  
**Timestamp:** 2026-07-14  
**1-line Summary:** Trump blockade + 20% cargo tax is policy escalation beyond military response; introduces regulatory/political risk to shipping costs.

**Tone:** BEARISH (policy uncertainty, supply-side headwind, stagflation risk)

**Rationale:** 20% cargo tax is de facto oil price tax; would permanently raise all commodity costs routed through Strait. If implemented and sustained, adds structural cost to global supply chains. Retaliation risk: Iran/China/Russia could impose counter-tariffs on US goods. This is political escalation, not just military, and harder to de-escalate (politics slower than shooting stops). **Long-term risk:** If Trump blockade / cargo tax becomes policy, energy costs structurally higher, inflation re-accelerates, Fed forced to tighten, equities correct.
